I came to data science from the lab bench — two years of turning raw instrument output and field samples into clean, analysis-ready data for environmental health research.

**At Duke University**, I worked in Dr. Heather Stapleton's exposure science lab, which studies human exposure to PFAS, brominated flame retardants, and organophosphate esters — especially in vulnerable populations like pregnant women, children, and firefighters. I cleaned and analyzed sample data across three research projects and helped recruit and consent 850 participants for the North Carolina Firefighter Cancer Cohort Study. Today I'm pursuing a **Master of Science in Applied Data Science at the University of Chicago**, building the engineering and statistical toolkit to do that same work at scale — and seeking a **Summer 2027 internship in data science, data engineering, or forward deployed engineering**.

---

## Highlights

**850 research participants recruited and consented** — for the North Carolina Firefighter Cancer Cohort Study (NC FFCCS), working directly at fire stations; collected, aliquoted, and stored biological samples under IRB and CITI protocols.

**Three research datasets cleaned and analyzed** — HHEAR (Human Health Exposure Analysis Resource), CURE (chronic kidney disease in agricultural communities), and HUD (housing and urban development).

**Methods adapted to the data, not the other way around** — tailored standard operating procedures to new sample types and target analytes; ran extractions, cleanup, and instrument setup for GC-MS detection.

**Confidential data handled end to end** — coordinated sample analysis with partner laboratories while following confidential data procedures for human-subjects research.

**Cloud data engineering, hands on** — data acquisition with Python (Requests, BeautifulSoup, pandas), Linux/Bash, Google Cloud, relational modeling in MySQL, and warehousing with Snowflake through UChicago's Cloud Native Data Engineering course.
